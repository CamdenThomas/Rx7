**D-306 — The ICU joins early, headless: a carrier with the analog front end, the Teensy and the CAN transceiver, plugged into `DP-ICU-A` / `DP-ICU-B` after the electrical build's shakedown, publishing on CAN2 while the factory cluster stays the instrument; the display and the dash plastics arrive later as a bezel-only event.** Camden's call, 2026-09-07, the module half of the electrical build's `Q-116` (its wiring half is electrical D-251). Every sensor is read once, at the ICU, and every other reader gets it from the bus: the PMU logs rpm, oil pressure, water temperature and fuel level as received channels from the day the node powers up, and D-183's rpm terms can come in years before a screen exists. D-081 holds — nothing that starts, runs or makes the car legal depends on this node — and D-251 forbids it ever becoming a control source: a CAN value in any PMU control rule fails open. Three consequences. **(1) Fuel level is this module's signal.** The electrical build re-pointed A7 at the oil-pressure node for the fuel-pump gate (D-249), so `DP-ICU-B 3` taps the fuel node and the ICU publishes it in a new slow frame, **`0x218` at 2 Hz** (`01-DESIGN/CAN-MESSAGES.md`); the PMU's `0x120` bytes 0–1 become reserved. Oil pressure stays in `0x200` — a lower identifier at 20 Hz — which is what "oil pressure outranks fuel level" means on a bus: priority is per frame, never per byte. **(2) D-078 is superseded and restated.** Single source per signal stands: the PMU owns battery voltage, key state, output states and channel currents; the ICU owns rpm, water temp, oil temp, oil pressure, road speed *and fuel level*; the one deliberate double read is the oil-pressure node, watched by the gauge, the PMU's A7 and the ICU, as a cross-check. **(3) The carrier's analog front end is built without the display** — easier, not harder — and its 12 V input stays on `DP-ICU-A 1` (O10) at Teensy-plus-transceiver draw until `Q-302` is measured with a panel attached.
