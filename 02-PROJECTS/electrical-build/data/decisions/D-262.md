**D-262 — The sensor inputs are built to outlive the 12A. Every ICU sender channel is a pull-up behind a jumper (fitted for the 12A's 2-wire resistive senders, open for a 0–5 V transducer), a ×0.4 divider that reads either, and a per-channel curve in config-as-data — never a board change; the PMU's A7 takes a resistive sender with the 100 Ω pull-up in (D-260) or a 0–5 V pressure transducer with it lifted at `N39`; the engine leg is cut at the grommet and rebuilt at the swap (D-211(d)), and what the new engine must deliver to these inputs is the hand-over table in `../engine-swap/README.md`.** Camden, 2026-09-07: "with the engine swap I will need to power the more complicated sensors" — and the bay stays stripped, so nothing is pre-wired for a sensor that does not exist. The 12A's senders are one-wire resistive parts the ICU excites; an LS's are 3-wire 5 V devices read by its own ECU, which publishes them on CAN2 (`0x500+`, the luxury map). So at the swap most ICU channels go quiet and the display reads the ECU's frames — a firmware rule, luxury `F-014`: local input when configured, else the ECU's frame, else dashes (luxury D-153) — while the two signals the *car* acts on stay on wire per D-251: oil pressure into A7 from a sender or transducer on the new block (never the ECU's CAN value), and the tach into `DP-ICU-B 4` from the ECU's tach output through the same clamp. `L1-S2 9` (switched 12 V, D-250) powers a 12 V-supplied 3-wire transducer if one is fitted; a 5 V sensor is the ECU's, or gets a regulator at the dash node — no +5 V goes down the leg (D-215). The three reserves `L1-S2 4–6` and the spare `L1-S2 11 → DP-ICU-B 9` are the only new-sensor conductors, and they already exist.
