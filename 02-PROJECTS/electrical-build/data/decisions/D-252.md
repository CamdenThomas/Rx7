**D-252 — The ICU drop is two housings, not one: `DP-ICU-A`, a DT-6 carrying power and bus, and `DP-ICU-B`, a DT-12 carrying only sensors.** Camden's call, 2026-09-07, answering `Q-117`. After the fuel tap (D-251) the single DT-12 had one cavity left, and the ICU's own `0x200` frame already expects oil temperature and road speed — neither had a conductor or a cavity. `DP-ICU-A` is cavity-for-cavity the DP-DCU pattern (1 +12 V switched off O10 · 3 ground · 4 CAN2 H · 5 CAN2 L) plus the illumination reference on 2 and a plug on 6, so every module in the car plugs into the same six-way and only the ICU has a second, sensor-only housing. `DP-ICU-B`: 1 water temp · 2 oil pressure · 3 fuel level · 4 tach · 5 charge sense · 6 brake warning — the six taps — then 7 oil temperature (`L1-S2 10`, D-250) · 8 road-speed signal (`L4-S 3`) · 9 Phase-2 spare (`L1-S2 11`) · 10–12 plugs. Road speed gets two 16 AWG conductors in L4 now — `L4-S 3` signal, `L4-S 4` sensor supply, capped in the tunnel at the transmission's speedo drive with a service loop — because no road-speed signal exists on this car (the speedometer is cable-driven) and L4 already runs the tunnel; if an inline sensor fits at the speedometer head behind the dash (checked at M-2) they stay capped forever and nothing is lost. A Hall or open-collector sensor is what they suit; a VR sensor would need a shielded pair that is not provided. *Old → new:* DP-ICU 1 → A 1 · 2 → A 3 · 3 → A 4 · 4 → A 5 · 5 → A 2 · 6 → B 1 · 7 → B 2 · 8 (plug) → B 3 fuel · 9 → B 4 · 10 (plug) → B 8 road speed · 11 → B 5 · 12 → B 6 — any older text citing a `DP-ICU n` reads through this table, and "the five drops" of D-017/D-037 are six housings. *Cart:* one DT06-6S / DT04-6P assembly-kit pair and a 6-way dust cap join the DeutschConnector line — a few dollars. *Supersedes* D-215's "DP-ICU is the 12-way sensor drop"; D-159 (the display never crosses the harness) is unchanged and is what makes the display a bezel-only event whenever the dash design is ready.
