**D-267 — The ICU carries a radio co-processor: an ESP32-C3 module on the carrier's 3.3 V rail, on a UART to the Teensy, decoding battery data over Bluetooth LE and republishing it as a CAN frame (`0x220`, 2 Hz — volts, amps, state of charge, temperature, flags) that the display draws and the PMU logs. Nothing at the drops or in the harness changes; the enclosure wall in front of its antenna must be RF-transparent, or the antenna goes outside (a constraint on `Q-126`). A dev board is bought now to try the decode (`P116`); which source it decodes — the Ionic's BMS, a shunt, or both — is `Q-128`.** Camden's call, 2026-09-07: "the board is cheap and worth buying to try; if it is an isolated addition inside the ICU case leaving everything the same, then definitely plan to add it." It is: channel `IC24`, two UART lines and two GPIOs on the board, ~120 mA on a rail budgeted at 0.6 A, no cavity, no conductor, no cart line beyond the module. The Teensy stays the ICU's brain (the reasoning is in Q-128: the ESP32's ADC, its render bandwidth, the Teensy-shaped firmware, three Teensys in hand); the radio's boot time and its FreeRTOS timing never touch the instrument core. Because the ICU's logic rail is O10 (accessory), the radio runs only with the key in — the overnight consumption is read at key-on from the BMS's or the shunt's own counters, which is what G4 needs. D-251 holds: the frame is telemetry; state of charge may enter the 11.5 V shed rule only as a secondary, fail-open term. The frame's layout is the luxury CAN map's (`0x220`); the firmware work is luxury `F-015`.
