**D-257 — The PMU's PWM is software-adjustable from 4 Hz to 400 Hz per output (Camden, from the manual, 2026-09-07), so the blower's speed control is the low-side final stage at `L3-BLW`, switching at ≥ 20 kHz on a command the DCU wires to it directly; O16 is a steady feed, ON in RUN.** A brushed blower chopped at 400 Hz hums at part speed — the PWM frequency and its harmonics come straight out of the motor and the ducting — and a silent cabin cannot have that, so what D-253 held as the fallback is the plan: module into `L3-BLW`, motor into the module, the DCU's PWM output to the module on a local pigtail. Nothing in the harness moves; that was the point of the receptacle. The module carries its own freewheel diode across the motor (the PMU's integrated diode clamps the high side only and does nothing for low-side chopping), a ≥ 25 A rating, a logic-level PWM or 0–5 V input, and mounts in the resistor pack's hole for airflow; an OEM-style linear blower regulator would also work but dissipates the difference as heat at mid speeds. `BLOWER` is `A16 >= RUN`, DISABLED until the motor exists; the `0x300` blower byte becomes what the DCU reports, not what the PMU applies. *Supersedes* D-253's control clause ("a duty command the DCU sends over CAN2 and the PMU applies on O16") and its fallback wording; D-253's wiring stands unchanged.
