Why there. The PMU box feeds the whole car, but most of its wires never leave the dash. The DU, the CU, the CUR, the column switches, the seats and the diagnostic port are all within reach, and each already has a plug at its own end, so those wires are hard-wired at the box. The engine, front and rear harnesses are different: each must come off with the parts it serves. The engine harness is replaced at the LS swap, and the front comes off with the nose. So each of those gets a plug on the box. Those plugs are what the dash post's 16 receptacles were, moved onto the box, and the jumper wires between the post and the node go.

Plug type: mixed-contact Deutsch circulars (D-600). Each direction carries three heavy 12 AWG circuits and 6 to 14 lighter ones. Engine: ignition and the two LS reserves. Front: both headlamps and the pop-up feed. Rear: defog, fuel pump and the window motor bus. A mixed-contact Deutsch circular (the HD30 / HDP20 family, size-12 and size-16 contacts in one shell ?, the exact insert to confirm) does each direction in one plug, two or three for the engine once it carries the swap's ECU harness. The break-point step (M7) can still add a split, at the firewall or the column, where installing needs one.

Design notes found in this pass

Going over the plan again turned these up. None changes a choice; each is something the board, the wire list or the mock-up has to get right.

The smart switches must reproduce the relays' control logic exactly

Each relay is switched today by one of two kinds of control.

  • High side: the coil is fed by a PMU output. K11 (O22) and K14 / K15 (O15) are this kind.
  • Low side: something grounds the coil's other end. K3 / K4 (the CU's release selects), K12 (the washer contact on the stalk) and K1 / K2 (through each wink's NC pole) are this kind.

The board turns each into its switch's input with one of two small circuits, so every switch does exactly what its relay did. The pop-ups need the most care. Each side becomes two switches (up to R, down to RY), interlocked so both are never on, and "up" follows the lamps' command while the wink pole is closed, as the relay did.

F24 stays after F3 inside the box

F24 (3 A) is fed from F3's output (5 A) on the board, so a light-side short still blows only F24 and the key's fuse stands (D-599).

The CAN chain and its two ends

CAN2 runs PMU box → DU (the gauge rides on it inside) → CU. Its ends are the PMU's own termination and a 120 Ω resistor in the CU, which takes over from the node's resistor (D-346). The diagnostic port is CAN1, the PMU's other bus, unchanged.

One ground star

Every return in the dash comes back to the PMU box's ground star: the DU, CU and CUR, the seat heat and cool returns, the seat motors, and the blower final stage's return from the HVAC case. The star's one heavy lead goes to a chassis ground point outside the box, where the body bond is best (D-603).

The oil-pressure line has two readers

The sender's wire comes to the PMU box (its pull-up and the PMU's A7, the fuel-pump gate, D-260), and the box sends it on to the DU. The tach, water temperature, fuel level, charge lamp, brake warning and road speed go straight to the DU.

Splices that stay wire

The head unit's reverse, road-speed and parking-brake taps are splices in the wire, not parts, and so is the horn pad sharing A8 with the CUR's wink and hazard lines. The wire list puts each splice where it can be reached.

Before the CAD: what is still to dial in

Each of these, missed, would send a mock-up or a board back to the start. The first four are settled (D-601 to D-603); 5 to 10 are mine and can start now.

1 · The fifth box: the LS swap's ECU (a), D-601

The ECU (the EMU PRO, engine D-446) is a dash node (D-318, D-325), so at the swap the dash gains a fifth box with about 72 engine wires. Engine D-443 planned them on six 12-way DT plugs at the dash post, and the post is gone (D-596). There are three ways to go:

  • (a) The ECU goes inside the PMU box: both are ECUMaster parts on the same CAN, and the box's engine plug grows to carry both. The fewest boxes and plugs, but a bigger box.
  • (b) The ECU is its own box beside the PMU box, and its engine harness runs from its own plugs.
  • (c) One bulkhead connector at the firewall that both engine harnesses pass through.

The PMU box's mock-up has to leave room for whichever it is.

2 · The two faces D-602

The CU face (eighth pass) and the CUR face (second pass) are still concept pages; the record holds the older layouts (D-595). Each board's outline and key positions follow its face, so both settle before their boards are redrawn.

3 · Where the grounds land D-603

Two returns are still open. The seat heat and cool returns can ground at each seat or come back to the CU. The blower final stage's return can go to the PMU box's star or a stud at the HVAC case. A ground is safety, so it is your call; I would bring both back to the star, so the dash keeps one ground point.

4 · Measure the space (C1) assumed, D-603

The space under the dash, the binnacle's depth and the AFR sensor lead's length. Also where the PMU box can sit: its lid reachable for the fuses, its three plugs facing down or sideways (the water rule), and close to where the engine harness crosses the firewall.

5 · The PMU box's heat mine

A sealed box with the PMU, eight smart switches and about twenty fuses inside. The heat each one gives off at the record's currents decides whether the PMU wall is enough, or the box needs fins, vents or more size.

6 · The smart switches, proved mine

Pick the parts, draw the two control circuits and the pop-up interlock, and simulate them like the wake decks. That includes what each one does if it fails switched on: a release solenoid left powered must still be stopped by its fuse.

7 · The roadside recovery table mine

The steps of the PMU-fault recovery (D-322) and the glovebox kit's jumpers, checked against relays that are now switches on a board under a lid.

8 · Connector inserts, contacts and tools mine

The three circulars' inserts, with spare cavities for growth; whether they take the same contacts and crimper (P038) as the DT parts; and TE's word on four size-20 cavities in parallel for the CU's comfort feed.

9 · The price, before anything is drawn mine

The boxes' parts against what is in the carts today (nothing for the node is ordered yet), so you see the money before the CAD commits to it.

10 · The bench test of each box mine

Each box is built and proved whole on the bench before it goes in the car: continuity, isolation, then powered through a current limit with every output exercised.

11 · After the dash yours, later

The same idea fits the zones outside the dash: the sill node (the window relays, F8, F9, F14), the rear star node, and the engine bay's start relay and MIDI fuses. Not now; when the dash is drawn.

The next improvements

A second pass over the dash, with every answer so far in place. All settled (D-604, luxury D-605). Same rule as before: mine is a small call I can make; yours is money, safety, taste or one of your rulings.

N1 · The CU's box behind its short face (a), D-604

The 25.4 mm face cannot cover the box behind it. That box is about 50 mm tall because the carrier and its 64-way header stand about 49 mm (D-594).

  • (a) The box stays about 50 mm tall and steps up behind the dash, fitted from behind. No connector change, and the dash comes apart for the build anyway.
  • (b) The boards are laid out for a box about 25 mm tall, so the face covers it and it slides in from the front like the stereo. That needs a lower connector than the right-angle 64-way, which reopens D-594.

My pick: (a).

N2 · The CU's screen moves to the Display Unit declined, D-604

Drop the CU's small screen. The DU shows the climate setting large while a knob turns, then goes back to its page. The CU already sends its state on CAN.

  • Gain: about 41 mm of the face back, so bigger knobs and keys at 25.4 mm, and one part and its cable gone.
  • Cost: you read the set temperature on the cluster, not under your hand.

Taste, so yours. I'd try it.

N3 · The CUR to the CU on four wires, not twelve D-604

Put a small I/O expander and an I²C ADC on the CUR's board, so its keys and stick ride the I²C bus that already runs to it. The 12-line link becomes 4: data, clock, 5 V and ground. The five wink and hazard lines to the PMU box stay as they are, because they wake the car when it is asleep.

  • Gain: 8 fewer wires, and the CUR's plug could be a 12-way.
  • Cost: two small chips, about $3 ?, and firmware.

My pick: do it.

N4 · The ECU's feed never leaves the box D-604

With the ECU inside the PMU box (D-601), its own supply is wired inside the box. Corrected: O13 still leaves the box, because it feeds the injectors (E1-2), so the engine plugs keep all four heavy circuits.

N5 · At the swap, the DU's engine wires become CAN D-604

The EMU PRO reads rpm and water temperature itself and sends them on CAN (engine HO05 already plans rpm from the ECU). The DU's direct tach, water-temperature and oil-temperature wires go at the swap, freeing DU cavities. The DU's engine inputs are drawn as "until the swap".

N6 · At the swap, the AEM gauge may retire D-604, if it has one

ECUMaster's ECUs usually carry their own LSU 4.9 wideband controller ?; the EMU PRO's manual is not read yet (block APP.05). If it does, the sensor plugs into the ECU in the PMU box at the swap and the gauge comes out of the DU. It stays in the DU until then.

N7 · One diagnostic port for both ECUMaster units D-604, if it works

The glovebox port carries the PMU's configuration bus (CAN1). With the ECU in the same box, one port serves both, if the EMU PRO configures over the same kind of link ? (its manual again).

N8 · The head unit: maps, music and a microphone, no cameras luxury D-605

A head unit's reverse input only exists to switch its screen to a rear-view camera. With no camera it is not connected, so the screen never changes in reverse. The speed-pulse input feeds built-in navigation this unit does not have; its maps come from the phone over CarPlay and Android Auto, so it goes too (confirm in the manual). The parking-brake input stays, because such units lock some setup screens without it (confirm), and the microphone to the A-pillar stays as planned. Two fewer wires and two fewer splices in the dash.

The mock-up plan

In order. No CAD of any board until M6 passes.

  1. Measure the space (C1, yours, when the car is stripped). Until then the plan assumes it (D-603): the mock-ups are built to fit as drawn and checked when the numbers come. holds nothing up now
  2. Settle the choices (yours): done. D-597, D-598, D-599.
  3. Freeze each box's contents (mine). One list per box from the record, every item naming its row; F20 to 5 A and F24 to D-599 in the same pass.
  4. The box-to-box wire list (mine). Every conductor from today's node conductors, cavities and channels, rewritten from box to box: both ends, gauge and colour, the splices placed, the grounds to the star. The counts above become exact here.
  5. Parametric mock-ups (mine). FreeCAD, the same method as the climate unit, DU and CUR mock-ups:
    • the PMU box, new, in both shapes
    • the DU with the gauge and the gland
    • the CU with its feed, its seat switches and a stand-in for the radar receiver
    • the CUR on one DRC 24
    Each one is checked against C1 once measured.
  6. Try them in the car (yours). Printed or cardboard at real size under the dash. D-214's old worry, a sealed box too bulky for the footwell, is answered here, or the PMU box splits.
  7. Place the break points (together). With every wire placed, choose where runs split for installing and servicing, and any split beyond the PMU box's three plugs.
  8. Then the CAD (mine). The PMU box's board first: the fuses, the smart switches with their control circuits, K13, and the wake circuit. The wake circuit is proved by the existing simulations; its pulse capacitor becomes 4.7 µF X7R if ceramic, because 2.2 µF ceramic loses too much at 12 V and fails the cold corner. Then the CU's and the DU's changes.

Speaker wire through connectors: yes. A Deutsch contact adds a few thousandths of an ohm against a 4 Ω speaker, far below anything audible, and factory cars run speakers through two or three connectors. The low-level line signals are the sensitive ones: they ride in a shielded pair with the drain on its own cavity, grounded at one end. Docking the upmix on the amplifier keeps the digital link, which may be optical, from passing through any connector.

Zone 3 · The legs: a scan for loose parts

Every leg read device by device. Most of what the legs hold is already right: a device on the end of a wire, plus splices, which are wire. These are the parts that are not.

L1 · The sill node goes D-610

The last loose node in the car, under the driver's sill: the window relays K5–K8, three fuses (F8, F9, F14), five splices and a ground stud. The windows move to two H-bridges on the PMU box's board (30 A half-bridge class ?). The CU's four window commands, which went to the relay coils, go to those instead, a shorter run inside the dash. The door wires run from the boxes to D1 and D2, a short way through the A-pillar. F8 and F9 become the board's own protection; F14 goes into the CU, which already switches the mirror heat (DP-DCU 23).

  • Gain: the node, 4 relays, 3 fuses, 5 splices and a stud gone. The bridges can sense motor current, which makes one-touch and stop-on-stall possible later.
  • Cost: the PMU box's board grows by two bridges and their heat. It replaces D-363 (the relays as two reversing pairs).

L2 · The ladder resistors and diodes stop hanging in the harness D-610

Several switches carry their resistor or diode heat-shrunk into the leg:

  • the inhibitor's 8.2 kΩ pair
  • the wiper park's 12 kΩ, and its 47 kΩ at the L2-S plug
  • the door jambs' 24 kΩ / 8.2 kΩ with their 1N4148s and the lamp's 1N4007s (D-486)
  • the door ladder's 47 kΩ at the sill splice

Where a switch has its own wire to the box, its series resistor moves onto the PMU box's board. A resistor that must stay at the far end, so the PMU still sees a broken wire as a fault (the 47 kΩ baselines), is potted into a short sealed pigtail at its switch. That pigtail becomes part of the switch, not of the harness.

L3 · One plug per seat D-610, one plug

Each seat has two plugs today: L3-SD (4-way: heat and cool) and L3-SDM (2-way: power). That becomes one Deutsch DTP 4-way, size 12, 25 A: power feed, heat feed, cool feed and one common return to the PMU box's star (D-603). The seat comes out with one unplug.

  • Cost: three circuits share one 12 AWG return. That is about 32 A in the moment the seat moves with heat and cooling on, so the return is a ground choice and yours. The other way is two returns on a DT 6-way plus the power DTP, which is two plugs again.

L4 · The engine bay's small grounds D-610

K9's coil, the inhibitor and the brake fluid switch return to the engine block today on three 16 AWG drops. K9 is now inside the bay junction (D-609), so its ground is the junction's. The other two stay at the block: they are the switches' own ground on the engine.

L5 · Break points, settled at M7 last, at M7

Where a body part comes off with its wiring, one plug lets it go:

  • The nose at the core support: headlamps, pop-ups, turns, horns, wiper, washer, radar and outside air. The nose clip comes off with one unplug.
  • The hatch at its hinge: the defog grid, luggage lamp, release solenoid and rear radar.
  • The doors at the A-pillar: D1 and D2, already in the record.
