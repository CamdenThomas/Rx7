**Where a file goes.** A KiCad project or a FreeCAD model, and what is generated from it to be
built (the gerbers and their zip in `cad/<board>/fab/`, STEP, STL, FCStd), is `cad/`. Anything a
tool reads or writes as data - a BOM, a board's extract, a parameter table, a report - is `data/`.
Anything read by eye - SVG, PNG, HTML - is `diagrams/`. No file is in two places: a BOM exported
from a sheet, a fab BOM built from it by `fab_bom.py`, a cart written by `bench_cart.py` each say
in their tool which file they come from.

**The tools** (`tools/`, every one finds a part's files through `parts.py`, where the layout is
written once):

## 2 · The fence

**This is not the visual layer.** CLAUDE.md §1 and §9 say the tree has exactly one
generated document and that the visual layer of the record is a separate, later concern
that must not be started yet. Nothing in the CAD is generated, nothing in here renders the
record, and nothing in the drawings reads a CSV at build time. The CAD is a tool being used to
draw circuit boards, in the same category as the firmware — engineering artefacts for one
component, not a view of the design.

The fence, in full:

- **This folder is not an area.** No `data/_tables.csv`, so `rx7.py` does not see it
  at all: `areas()` cannot find it, `check` never reads it, and nothing here can make the
  record invalid or be made invalid by it. It sits in `01-electrical/`, beside `data/`, and
  never inside it (the CAD moved here 2026-09-23, D-386).
- **The boards live in their parts (D-496, D-497, D-611).** The Control Unit's (`control-unit/cad/`:
  the CU carrier, the face board and the unit's mock-up; the CUR's board and mock-up in
  `control-unit/control-unit-remote/cad/`) and the Display Unit's (`display-unit/cad/`: the DU
  carrier, the HDMI bridge and the unit's mock-up) are drawn under this same fence; `tools/` keeps
  every script and `data/` what the parts share.
- **The record wins, always.** If a schematic and a row disagree, the row is right and the
  drawing is stale. Nothing in `../data/` cites a file in here as evidence.
- **A fact worth keeping is promoted, not linked** — it becomes a row, and the drawing
  becomes an illustration of the row.
- **Who draws (D-453).** Camden ruled on 2026-09-12 that he would draw these himself to learn
  KiCad; D-453 lifts that, and the agent draws, lays out and checks the boards too. They are
  still not a view layer: if one starts to look like it, delete it, don't grow it.
- **The firmware is a source of truth** (R6). `display-unit/firmware/du/cluster_core.h` defines
  the palette, every layout constant, the icon set, unit conversions and sensor-fault rendering
  (D-151…D-158). `display-unit/firmware/du/stats.h` owns the automated trip and lifetime figures
  (D-163). `display-unit/firmware/du/can_map.h` is the machine-readable CAN map. The prose
  documents describe *why*; this code defines *what*. It owns, in code: cluster layout, palette,
  rendering, CAN structs and the automated trip figures.

## 5 · Why a schematic at all

The analog front end is currently only describable in prose. `icu_channels.csv` row IC01
carries, in its stage column, *"100 Ω 1 W pull-up from the carrier's 5 V behind jumper J1
(fitted) → node → 15 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider (×0.4, 5 V → 2.0 V) → 100 nF → BAT54S to 3V3 /
GND"*. That is precise, complete, and unreviewable — nobody can read that sentence and see
that a clamp is on the wrong side of a resistor. Drawn, it is one glance.

That is the whole argument, and it is narrow. It does not extend to the harness: wire
gauge, colour, bundles, cavities and cut lengths are the record's job and KiCad has no
model for any of them.

And it earned that on the first attempt, which belongs here where the argument is made: drawing
IC01 turned up that **every BAT54S clamp on the board was the wrong way round** - anode to +3V3 and
cathode to ground. The sentence above is silent on direction and reads perfectly either way round.
The picture is not.

## 6 · Checking the sheets against the record (`tools/check.py`, Y3)

`python3 tools/check.py` (from this folder, or from anywhere; `-v` for every pass and the walk it took, `--board du-carrier|cu-carrier|cu-face-board|cur-board`, `--netlists DIR` to reuse exports) runs `kicad-cli sch export netlist` on each sheet, one at a time, and holds the drawing to the record: every pin in `icu_channels.teensy_pin` and `dcu_channels.teensy_pin` on its socket pad and named net, reaching the part the record says (the XIAO's TX, the transceiver's STB, the expander's ports); every `at_the_drop` cavity wired on the board-edge connector and walked through the board to that channel's processor pin; the ribbon against `panel_ribbon` on both boards, with the keys, encoders and thumbstick pins that hang off each conductor and the matrix diodes' direction; `CONVENTIONS.md` §5 (the rails never meet, every BAT54S the right way round); and each functional-block symbol's pin numbers against the vendor pin tables in `data/pin_tables.csv` (part, package, pin, name, source URL and page). On the upper connector board (archived 2026-10-15), which had no processor, every cavity of `DP-DCU-D` and `DP-DCU-E` is held to its `state` (wired, or bare behind a plug), to a net of its own, and to what its `src_note` names: the header for the carrier's J6-J8 / J5 / J3 lead pin for pin, or the `panel_ribbon` line, named as that line. It prints one `board:ref:pin expected X got Y` line per disagreement and a summary line per board; exit 0 clean, 1 any mismatch, 3 kicad-cli missing or a sheet unreadable. `tests/run.sh` runs it after the can_map check, so a schematic that disagrees with the record fails the test suite (skipped with a notice when kicad-cli is absent). The record wins: a mismatch is fixed on the sheet, or in `data/pin_tables.csv` from the datasheet, never by editing the check to pass.

**Transistors and diodes are held to a pin table too (Y42, 2026-10-13).** Pricing the bench order turned up three of one kind on the two carriers, each found by hand: the DCU's Q3 drawn gate / drain / source on a SOT-23 (every SOT-23 FET is gate, source, drain), the ICU's zener D20 with its cathode on the package's unused pin, and six one-way TVS diodes drawn with two anodes, the footprint's cathode mark on ground. So a `Q` is checked against `pin_tables.csv` as a `U` is, with a note if it has no table, and a `D` is checked when a table names its value (the one-way TVS: pin 1 the cathode, the end KiCad's footprint marks). Run on the sheets as they were, it refuses all three (the old Q3 only once its part is named). Counts with it, 2026-10-15: du-carrier 105, cu-carrier 172, cu-face-board 79, cur-board 31.

The left plate's wink check (`check_winks`, Y22, D-499 / D-504) is read from the record too: `J2`'s pins against the `L3-S1` cavities that land on J2 (each wink's NC and common on the pin the row names), the commons off the plate's GND and every J1 net, and one resistor from each switch's NO to J2 pin 3 at the value cavity 11's text gives. No number is typed in it; swapped pins and a wrong value were each refused when tried.

First run, 2026-10-09: every record pin on every sheet matched. The 106 mismatches it reports are all pin-table ones on the DCU - U5-U8 BTS3011TE, U9 BTT6050, U10 BTT6200 and U11 DRV8962 still carry functional-block pin numbers (Y5 redraws them on their real packages) - and the ICU's U8 XIAO, whose symbol has EN and BOOT pins the module does not expose as castellations (IC24).

**`can_map.h` has two copies** (`display-unit/firmware/du/`, `control-unit/firmware/cu/`) because
the Arduino IDE needs the header beside each sketch. **`icu/can_map.h` is the master.** When it
changes, copy it over `dcu/`. `tests/run.sh` refuses to run the suites while the two differ (plan
P49, 2026-09-26).

**The log link (D-478, D-493, D-494, BT3).** One wire, Teensy Serial2 ↔ C3 Serial1 at 921600,
carries the `$BAT` / `$CELLS` lines and the binary frames of `log_link.h` (a frame starts `0xAA`,
a line `$`). The phone writes one characteristic; the C3 checks the opcode (six of them) and
forwards it as a frame; the Teensy answers from the card (`log_store.h`) and streams `DATA`
only as far as the C3's `CREDIT`. A tier is marked pulled in `pulled.flg` only on an `ACK`
whose CRC-32 matches, and nothing is deleted on the app's word. Nothing over the link reaches
an output, a setting or the flash; `SET_TIME` (unset, or off by more than 2 s) is the one write.
Four frame types (`PAIRING 0x21`, `START 0x85`, `PAIR 0x86`, `UNBOND 0x87`) and `err 7` are
added beside the record's, and named in `log_link.h`'s header. Unproven until BT4: the C3 as
scanner, BMS client and peripheral at once, the rate, and a bonded phone's private address
through the whitelist.

**The DCU's pins are `pins.h`, and `pins.h` is `dcu_channels.teensy_pin`** (D-452). All 42 edge
pins are used, none of the bottom pads. The slow lines go through a TCA9539-Q1 at 0x74 on Wire
(SDA 18, SCL 19, INT 12, RESET 13): the DRV8962's IN1–3, EN1–4 and nSLEEP, its nFAULT, the
PROFETs' DEN / DSEL, the transceiver's STB and the buck's power-good. STB is written low at boot,
before CAN2 starts, and no output word can raise it. The expander's RESET is the boot path and
also the DRV8962's latched over-current clear: every port turns input, nSLEEP falls on its
pull-down, and the driver wakes clean. A clear needs the stick let go and a 1 s rest, three per key
cycle. `test_dcu` sections 14–17 hold the map, the ports, the reset and STB. A pin moved in the
record is moved in `pins.h` and in the test, and `tools/check.py` holds the sheet to the same row.

**`sim_sdl.cpp` includes `../du/cluster_core.h` directly** — one source of
truth, no second copy to drift. **`channels.h` is not generated any more:** the v2 build that rendered it from the electrical pin table (D-311) is archived, so a pin row changed in `02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/data/pins.csv` is copied into `channels.h` by hand (work F-013).

## 10 · What the renderer guarantees

**Compose freely, transmit sparingly.** The 614 KB framebuffer lives in the
8 MB PSRAM (D-170 / D-193); only changed 16 × 16 tiles go over QSPI to the
BT817 (D-168). A full-screen push is ~100 ms — fine as a page transition,
fatal inside a 30 fps loop.

**Never call anything that clears the whole screen in a redraw path.** That
single discipline is what makes the design work.

**Every widget diffs its own state.** A digit redraws only the segments that
changed; a bar redraws only the segments that lit or unlit; an unchanged
element costs zero pixels.

**A missing sensor never renders as a real zero** (D-153). Open, short, stale
and out-of-range each have their own look.

## 12 · The bench mule — what three Teensys buy you

With the PMU simulator, the ICU can be developed and demonstrated
**completely without the car**:

```
   [Teensy 1: ICU]  <--- CAN2 ---  [Teensy 2: pmu_sim]
        |  ^                        fuel, volts, key state,
        |  |                        24 channels, faults
        |  +--- pulse in ---------  [Teensy 3: tach_simulator]
        |                           RPM, and VSS on a second channel
        |
        +--- analog in ---------->  potentiometer
                                    stands in for a sender
```

**The DCU on this bench refuses every hatch and fuel-door release with the key on** (D-465): it
needs a fresh 0x200 reading 0 km/h, and nothing sends 0x200 yet - not `du.ino`, not `pmu_sim`.
With `pmu_sim` reporting the key OFF it allows them (D-484), which is the key-off case, not a
stand-in for the speed.

**What that covers:** the CAN receive path, message dispatch, timeout and
blanking, the diagnostics page, `stats.h` accumulation, the RPM capture path,
ladder decode, and the whole rendering layer. Needs two SN65HVD230 modules
with their headers soldered, a twisted pair and 120 Ω × 2 — the S0 lines of
`../data/parts.csv` (the 120 Ω are electrical P070's spares).

**What it does not cover — and this is the important limit:** the ICU's
critical gauges are on **its own analog inputs, not CAN** (D-083). The PMU
simulator exercises only the four things that genuinely come from the PMU:
battery voltage, key state and channel telemetry — fuel level is the ICU's
own input since electrical D-249 / D-306. Pull the PMU sim's power and watch:
volts should blank, everything else should carry on. **If the whole cluster goes blank, the failure isolation D-083
promises is not actually there.**

**It is not the PMU.** ECUMaster fixes the real message structure, and
`V-065` still has to happen. **The amp numbers are estimates** — 19 of 22
outputs at the last count; the simulator prints the number at boot.

## 13 · The design in 3D for the app (`tools/export_glb.py`, D-505, Y23)

The app's Design page (02-APP M3D1) shows this folder in 3D: every unit, every board, the PMU and
one labelled line per housing. It draws nothing itself; one command exports what is drawn here:

```
python3 02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/00-design/tools/export_glb.py               build all (about 1 min)
python3 02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/00-design/tools/export_glb.py --only panel   one asset, then scene.json

python3 02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/00-design/tools/export_glb.py --selftest     hold scene.json to the contract
```

From the tree's root (`--out DIR` for elsewhere; on the Mac, `env DEVELOPER_DIR=...` as every
python3 call). Exit 0 built / current; 1 a source is inconsistent (a mock-up refuses its
parameters, a board the scene names has no glb, a stale asset under `--check`, a scene that
breaks the contract); 2 under `--check`, assets not built on this machine; 3 a usage error or a
tool crash. It needs `kicad-cli` (KiCad 10) and `freecadcmd` (FreeCAD 1.1); `KICAD_CLI`,
`FREECADCMD` and `KICAD10_3DMODEL_DIR` override where it looks. `RX7_MODELS` is set for it.

**What it writes**, into `02-APP/app/public/design/01-electrical/`:

| File | From | How |
|---|---|---|
| `cu-carrier.glb`, `cu-face-board.glb`, `cur-board.glb`, `du-carrier.glb` | each board's `.kicad_pcb` | `kicad-cli pcb export glb --subst-models` with tracks, pads, zones, silkscreen and soldermask; two nodes, `pcb:board` and `pcb:parts`. DNP parts are drawn (the worst case the mock-ups check). Copper and mask edges under 0.1 mm tall are dropped: invisible, and half the copper's vertices |
| `control-unit.glb`, `display-unit.glb` | the mock-ups, `cu-assembly` and `du-assembly` | `export_glb_fc.py` calls the mock-up's own library `tools/<assembly>.py` (derive, validate, build_insides, build_case - what `tools/mockup.py` calls), so the geometry is the mock-up's and no number is typed twice. One node per solid: `case:<part>` and `inside:<name>`. The solids a board's glb stands for (the carrier and the face board) and the keep-outs are left out |
| `pmu.glb` | `pmu/vendor/PMU CAD info/PMU-24.STEP` | read by FreeCAD, each face coloured as the STEP colours it, meshed at 0.03 mm and 0.15 rad (`PMU_LINEAR`, `PMU_ANGULAR`); one node per vendor part, `part:<label>` |

positions, 8-bit normals (`KHR_mesh_quantization`, which three.js reads without a decoder).
three.js strips `:` and spaces from node names (`case:lid` becomes `caselid`); the name as
written is in `object.userData.name`.
