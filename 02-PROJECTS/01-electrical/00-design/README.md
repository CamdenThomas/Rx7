## 2 · The fence

**This is not the visual layer.** CLAUDE.md §1 and §9 say the tree has exactly one
generated document and that the visual layer of the record is a separate, later concern
that must not be started yet. Nothing in the CAD is generated, nothing in here renders the
record, and nothing in the drawings reads a CSV at build time. The CAD is a tool being used to
draw circuit boards, in the same category as the firmware — engineering artefacts for one
component, not a view of the design.

The fence, in full:

- **This folder is not an area.** No `data/`, no `_tables.csv`, so `rx7.py` does not see it
  at all: `areas()` cannot find it, `check` never reads it, and nothing here can make the
  record invalid or be made invalid by it. It sits in `01-electrical/`, beside `data/`, and
  never inside it (the CAD moved here 2026-09-23, D-386).
- **The boards live in their parts (D-496, D-497).** The Control Unit's (`control-unit/cad/`: the
  DCU carrier, the panel, the upper board, the left plate, the climate-unit mock-up) and the
  Display Unit's (`display-unit/cad/`: the ICU carrier, the icu-unit mock-up) are drawn under this
  same fence; `tools/` keeps what they all use - the guide, `check.py` with `pin_tables.csv`, and
  the enclosure library.
- **The record wins, always.** If a schematic and a row disagree, the row is right and the
  drawing is stale. Nothing in `../data/` cites a file in here as evidence.
- **A fact worth keeping is promoted, not linked** — it becomes a row, and the drawing
  becomes an illustration of the row.
- **Who draws (D-453).** Camden ruled on 2026-09-12 that he would draw these himself to learn
  KiCad; D-453 lifts that, and the agent draws, lays out and checks the boards too. They are
  still not a view layer: if one starts to look like it, delete it, don't grow it.
- **The firmware is a source of truth** (R6). `display-unit/firmware/icu/cluster_core.h` defines
  the palette, every layout constant, the icon set, unit conversions and sensor-fault rendering
  (D-151…D-158). `display-unit/firmware/icu/stats.h` owns the automated trip and lifetime figures
  (D-163). `display-unit/firmware/icu/can_map.h` is the machine-readable CAN map. The prose
  documents describe *why*; this code defines *what*. It owns, in code: cluster layout, palette,
  rendering, CAN structs and the automated trip figures.

## 5 · Why a schematic at all

The analog front end is currently only describable in prose. `icu_channels.csv` row IC01
carries, in its stage column, *"100 Ω 1 W pull-up from the carrier's 5 V behind jumper J1
(fitted) → node → 15 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider (×0.4, 5 V → 2.0 V) → 100 nF → BAT54S to 3V3 /
GND"*. That is precise, complete, and unreviewable — nobody can read that sentence and see
that a clamp is on the wrong side of a resistor. Drawn, it is one glance.

That is the whole argument, and it is narrow. It does not extend to the harness: wire
gauge, colour, bundles, cavities and cut lengths are the record's job and KiCad has no
model for any of them.

And it earned that on the first attempt, which belongs here where the argument is made: drawing
IC01 turned up that **every BAT54S clamp on the board was the wrong way round** - anode to +3V3 and
cathode to ground. The sentence above is silent on direction and reads perfectly either way round.
The picture is not.

## 6 · Checking the sheets against the record (`tools/check.py`, Y3)

`python3 tools/check.py` (from this folder, or from anywhere; `-v` for every pass and the walk it took, `--board icu|dcu|panel|upper|left-plate`, `--netlists DIR` to reuse exports) runs `kicad-cli sch export netlist` on each sheet, one at a time, and holds the drawing to the record: every pin in `icu_channels.teensy_pin` and `dcu_channels.teensy_pin` on its socket pad and named net, reaching the part the record says (the XIAO's TX, the transceiver's STB, the expander's ports); every `at_the_drop` cavity wired on the board-edge connector and walked through the board to that channel's processor pin; the ribbon against `panel_ribbon` on both boards, with the keys, encoders and thumbstick pins that hang off each conductor and the matrix diodes' direction; `CONVENTIONS.md` §5 (the rails never meet, every BAT54S the right way round); and each functional-block symbol's pin numbers against the vendor pin tables in `tools/pin_tables.csv` (part, package, pin, name, source URL and page). On the upper connector board, which has no processor, every cavity of `DP-DCU-D` and `DP-DCU-E` is held to its `state` (wired, or bare behind a plug), to a net of its own, and to what its `src_note` names: the header for the carrier's J6-J8 / J5 / J3 lead pin for pin, or the `panel_ribbon` line, named as that line. It prints one `board:ref:pin expected X got Y` line per disagreement and a summary line per board; exit 0 clean, 1 any mismatch, 3 kicad-cli missing or a sheet unreadable. `tests/run.sh` runs it after the can_map check, so a schematic that disagrees with the record fails the test suite (skipped with a notice when kicad-cli is absent). The record wins: a mismatch is fixed on the sheet, or in `tools/pin_tables.csv` from the datasheet, never by editing the check to pass.

The left plate's wink check (`check_winks`, Y22, D-499 / D-504) is read from the record too: `J2`'s pins against the `L3-S1` cavities that land on J2 (each wink's NC and common on the pin the row names), the commons off the plate's GND and every J1 net, and one resistor from each switch's NO to J2 pin 3 at the value cavity 11's text gives. No number is typed in it; swapped pins and a wrong value were each refused when tried.

First run, 2026-10-09: every record pin on every sheet matched. The 106 mismatches it reports are all pin-table ones on the DCU - U5-U8 BTS3011TE, U9 BTT6050, U10 BTT6200 and U11 DRV8962 still carry functional-block pin numbers (Y5 redraws them on their real packages) - and the ICU's U8 XIAO, whose symbol has EN and BOOT pins the module does not expose as castellations (IC24).

**`can_map.h` has two copies** (`display-unit/firmware/icu/`, `control-unit/firmware/dcu/`) because
the Arduino IDE needs the header beside each sketch. **`icu/can_map.h` is the master.** When it
changes, copy it over `dcu/`. `tests/run.sh` refuses to run the suites while the two differ (plan
P49, 2026-09-26).

**The log link (D-478, D-493, D-494, BT3).** One wire, Teensy Serial2 ↔ C3 Serial1 at 921600,
carries the `$BAT` / `$CELLS` lines and the binary frames of `log_link.h` (a frame starts `0xAA`,
a line `$`). The phone writes one characteristic; the C3 checks the opcode (six of them) and
forwards it as a frame; the Teensy answers from the card (`log_store.h`) and streams `DATA`
only as far as the C3's `CREDIT`. A tier is marked pulled in `pulled.flg` only on an `ACK`
whose CRC-32 matches, and nothing is deleted on the app's word. Nothing over the link reaches
an output, a setting or the flash; `SET_TIME` (unset, or off by more than 2 s) is the one write.
Four frame types (`PAIRING 0x21`, `START 0x85`, `PAIR 0x86`, `UNBOND 0x87`) and `err 7` are
added beside the record's, and named in `log_link.h`'s header. Unproven until BT4: the C3 as
scanner, BMS client and peripheral at once, the rate, and a bonded phone's private address
through the whitelist.

**The DCU's pins are `pins.h`, and `pins.h` is `dcu_channels.teensy_pin`** (D-452). All 42 edge
pins are used, none of the bottom pads. The slow lines go through a TCA9539-Q1 at 0x74 on Wire
(SDA 18, SCL 19, INT 12, RESET 13): the DRV8962's IN1–3, EN1–4 and nSLEEP, its nFAULT, the
PROFETs' DEN / DSEL, the transceiver's STB and the buck's power-good. STB is written low at boot,
before CAN2 starts, and no output word can raise it. The expander's RESET is the boot path and
also the DRV8962's latched over-current clear: every port turns input, nSLEEP falls on its
pull-down, and the driver wakes clean. A clear needs the stick let go and a 1 s rest, three per key
cycle. `test_dcu` sections 14–17 hold the map, the ports, the reset and STB. A pin moved in the
record is moved in `pins.h` and in the test, and `tools/check.py` holds the sheet to the same row.

**`sim_sdl.cpp` includes `../icu/cluster_core.h` directly** — one source of
truth, no second copy to drift. **`channels.h` is not generated any more:** the v2 build that rendered it from the electrical pin table (D-311) is archived, so a pin row changed in `02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/data/pins.csv` is copied into `channels.h` by hand (work F-013).

## 9 · Building for the Teensy

Run the suites first (`tests/README.md`). Open `display-unit/firmware/icu/icu.ino` in the Arduino
IDE. Board: Teensy 4.1, CPU 600 MHz.
Library: **ACAN_T4**, not FlexCAN_T4 — only ACAN_T4 exposes loopback cleanly.

On Linux the upload needs PJRC's udev rule once, or the Teensy loader cannot see the
board (see pjrc.com/teensy/td_download.html for the current file); on the Mac nothing is
needed beyond Teensyduino or the `arduino-cli` Teensy core:

```
sudo curl -o /etc/udev/rules.d/00-teensy.rules https://www.pjrc.com/teensy/00-teensy.rules
sudo udevadm control --reload-rules && sudo udevadm trigger
```

**Do not put the simulator sources in the sketch folder** — the Arduino IDE
compiles every `.cpp` it finds there and will try to build the SDL host for
ARM.

`pushDirtyTiles()` in `icu.ino` is **the only display-dependent function in
the project.** It calls `display.pushDirty` (bt817.h), finished against the BT817 eval board on the bench (work F3 and F-008; D-314 → D-374).

## 10 · What the renderer guarantees

**Compose freely, transmit sparingly.** The 614 KB framebuffer lives in the
8 MB PSRAM (D-170 / D-193); only changed 16 × 16 tiles go over QSPI to the
BT817 (D-168). A full-screen push is ~100 ms — fine as a page transition,
fatal inside a 30 fps loop.

**Never call anything that clears the whole screen in a redraw path.** That
single discipline is what makes the design work.

**Every widget diffs its own state.** A digit redraws only the segments that
changed; a bar redraws only the segments that lit or unlit; an unchanged
element costs zero pixels.

**A missing sensor never renders as a real zero** (D-153). Open, short, stale
and out-of-range each have their own look.

## 11 · The bench stages (superseded sketches)

`can_map_test`, `can_loopback_test`, `ladder_decode_test` and `tach_simulator`
were the Stage 2–5 bring-up steps. **All passed.** The full ICU firmware has
absorbed their function. `ladder_decode_test` (Stage 4, `pmu/firmware/`, the PMU's ladder windows)
and `tach_simulator` (Stage 5, `display-unit/firmware/`, the display unit's RPM input) are kept
because each is a working, isolated test rig — the tach simulator in particular is still the
fastest way to feed an RPM signal to a board with one jumper wire.

`cluster_render_test` is the first renderer, written against a counting mock
canvas before `cluster_core.h` existed. It is superseded and not maintained;
it stays only as the record of how the pixel budget was first measured.

## 12 · The bench mule — what three Teensys buy you

With the PMU simulator, the ICU can be developed and demonstrated
**completely without the car**:

```
   [Teensy 1: ICU]  <--- CAN2 ---  [Teensy 2: pmu_sim]
        |  ^                        fuel, volts, key state,
        |  |                        24 channels, faults
        |  +--- pulse in ---------  [Teensy 3: tach_simulator]
        |                           RPM, and VSS on a second channel
        |
        +--- analog in ---------->  potentiometer
                                    stands in for a sender
```

**The DCU on this bench refuses every hatch and fuel-door release with the key on** (D-465): it
needs a fresh 0x200 reading 0 km/h, and nothing sends 0x200 yet - not `icu.ino`, not `pmu_sim`.
With `pmu_sim` reporting the key OFF it allows them (D-484), which is the key-off case, not a
stand-in for the speed.

**What that covers:** the CAN receive path, message dispatch, timeout and
blanking, the diagnostics page, `stats.h` accumulation, the RPM capture path,
ladder decode, and the whole rendering layer. Needs two SN65HVD230 modules
with their headers soldered, a twisted pair and 120 Ω × 2 — the S0 lines of
`../data/parts.csv` (the 120 Ω are electrical P070's spares).

**What it does not cover — and this is the important limit:** the ICU's
critical gauges are on **its own analog inputs, not CAN** (D-083). The PMU
simulator exercises only the four things that genuinely come from the PMU:
battery voltage, key state and channel telemetry — fuel level is the ICU's
own input since electrical D-249 / D-306. Pull the PMU sim's power and watch:
volts should blank, everything else should carry on. **If the whole cluster goes blank, the failure isolation D-083
promises is not actually there.**

**It is not the PMU.** ECUMaster fixes the real message structure, and
`V-065` still has to happen. **The amp numbers are estimates** — 19 of 22
outputs at the last count; the simulator prints the number at boot.

## 13 · The design in 3D for the app (`tools/export_glb.py`, D-505, Y23)

The app's Design page (02-APP M3D1) shows this folder in 3D: every unit, every board, the PMU and
one labelled line per housing. It draws nothing itself; one command exports what is drawn here:

```
python3 02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/00-design/tools/export_glb.py               build all (about 1 min)
python3 02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/00-design/tools/export_glb.py --only panel   one asset, then scene.json
python3 02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/00-design/tools/export_glb.py --only scene   scene.json alone, from the record
python3 02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/00-design/tools/export_glb.py --check        are the assets current?
python3 02-PROJECTS/01-electrical/00-design/tools/export_glb.py --selftest     hold scene.json to the contract
```

From the tree's root (`--out DIR` for elsewhere; on the Mac, `env DEVELOPER_DIR=...` as every
python3 call). Exit 0 built / current; 1 a source is inconsistent (a mock-up refuses its
parameters, a board the scene names has no glb, a stale asset under `--check`, a scene that
breaks the contract); 2 under `--check`, assets not built on this machine; 3 a usage error or a
tool crash. It needs `kicad-cli` (KiCad 10) and `freecadcmd` (FreeCAD 1.1); `KICAD_CLI`,
`FREECADCMD` and `KICAD10_3DMODEL_DIR` override where it looks. `RX7_MODELS` is set for it.

**What it writes**, into `02-APP/app/public/design/01-electrical/`:

| File | From | How |
|---|---|---|
| `dcu-carrier.glb`, `panel.glb`, `upper-board.glb`, `left-plate.glb`, `icu-carrier.glb` | each board's `.kicad_pcb` | `kicad-cli pcb export glb --subst-models` with tracks, pads, zones, silkscreen and soldermask; two nodes, `pcb:board` and `pcb:parts`. DNP parts are drawn (the worst case the mock-ups check). Copper and mask edges under 0.1 mm tall are dropped: invisible, and half the copper's vertices |
| `control-unit.glb`, `display-unit.glb` | the mock-ups, `climate-unit` and `icu-unit` | `export_glb_fc.py` calls the mock-up's own `unit_lib` (derive, validate, build_insides, build_case - what `make_unit.py` calls), so the geometry is the mock-up's and no number is typed twice. One node per solid: `case:<part>` and `inside:<name>`. The solids a board's glb stands for (the carrier, the panel board, the upper board and its receptacles) and the keep-outs are left out |
| `pmu.glb` | `pmu/vendor/PMU CAD info/PMU-24.STEP` | read by FreeCAD, each face coloured as the STEP colours it, meshed at 0.03 mm and 0.15 rad (`PMU_LINEAR`, `PMU_ANGULAR`); one node per vendor part, `part:<label>` |
| `scene.json` | the record and the assets | every part, place, connector and link (below) |

Every glb is in **millimetres, z up, in the part's own frame** - not glTF's metres and y up - so
the viewer hangs each file under a node at its `place` and nothing else; `frame.glb` in the scene
says so. The meshes are re-packed: one primitive per material per 65535 vertices, float
positions, 8-bit normals (`KHR_mesh_quantization`, which three.js reads without a decoder).
three.js strips `:` and spaces from node names (`case:lid` becomes `caselid`); the name as
written is in `object.userData.name`.

**The sizes and git.** 47.6 MB the set (2026-10-13, scene version 2): dcu-carrier 13.46 MB,
icu-carrier 9.21, devices 8.30 (48 files), panel 3.95, pmu 3.55, connectors 3.33 (18 files),
upper-board 2.44, left-plate 1.98, control-unit 0.41, display-unit 0.38, cur 0.26, scene.json
0.34. Over the 20 MB D-505 allowed in git, so the `.glb` files are ignored and rebuilt
with the first command above on each machine before the app is built; `scene.json` is committed.
A board's 3D model that this machine's KiCad library lacks is listed in `missing`, not a failure
(on the Mac: U1 and U11 on the DCU carrier, U2 on the panel, U1 on the ICU carrier).

**scene.json, version 1** (the contract M3D1 is built against; only add fields, never change one):

- `parts[]`: `id` (kebab-case), `name` (the record's words), `kind` (`unit`, `pcb`, `module`, or
  `node` - a place with no model, such as a leg's far end), `glb` (relative, null for a node),
  `bytes`, `parent` (a board inside a unit names it), `place` (x y z mm, rx ry rz degrees, three.js
  Euler XYZ; the car's frame, D-418, for a top-level part, the parent's frame for a child),
  `place_from` (`mock-up report`, `00-CAR zones <id>`, `00-CAR parts <id>` or `provisional`),
  `confirm`, `size_mm` (the glb's box, measured), `origin` (where the glb's origin sits),
  `connectors[]` (`code`, `at` in the part's frame, `face`, `note`), `children`, `source`, `nodes`
  (the named node groups). Added: `place_note`, saying what a provisional place stands for.
- `links[]`: one per housing - `id` and `code` (the housing's code), `from` / `to` (`part`,
  `connector`), `label` (code, family, ways), `ways`, `carries` (the cavities' circuits in cavity
  order), `confirm`.
- `missing[]`: what has no model and why. `notes[]`: what the viewer should say plainly.
- Added: `frame.glb`, `frame.rotation`, `assets` (each asset's build facts and source stamp, what
  `--only` and `--check` read) and `record_stamp`.

**Where things are, and what is judgement.** The children's places come from the mock-up's own
derive(): the carriers by the move the mock-up gives their STEP (a half turn for the DCU's), the
panel board on the mock-up's panel block (stood up, top side to the cabin), the upper board on
its block (its two receptacles land on the mock-up's cut-outs; the export notes it if they do
not). Nothing in the record places the units, the PMU or the dash post (00-CAR parts has no
`model_at_mm` for any of them; D-416), so `TOP` in the script puts each inside 00-CAR's `dash`
zone box by fractions - the centre stack, the binnacle, the dash node, left of the wheel - and
`WHERE` puts each housing's far end inside its zone (one line each). All of those are
`provisional` and `confirm` until the dash is measured (C4, V-102, V-113). The links: a housing on
a unit's rear wall runs to the PMU, which stands for the dash node (modules PMU), unless
`FAR_END` says otherwise (DP-DCU-D to the HVAC case, D-480; DP-DCU-E to the left plate, D-492); a
housing at the dash post runs from the PMU to its leg's far end; any other receptacle runs from
the PMU to its `where`. The post's receptacles are not modelled; their links start spread along
the PMU's socket face.

**scene.json, version 2** (D-514, Y26; the contract M3D2 is built against). Version 1 stands
whole - every field above, the links unchanged - and version 2 only adds:

- `parts[]` of `kind: "connector"`, two per housings row: `id` `<housing>:plug` or
  `<housing>:receptacle`, `name` (`L3-S1 plug · DT06-12S`), `family` (the variant:
  `housings.leg_side` for the plug, `box_side` for the receptacle), `half`, `housing`, `leg` (the
  housings.leg text), `glb` (`connectors/<variant>.glb`, from connectors/manifest.json), `bytes`,
  `parent` (the unit for a unit's receptacles, else null), `place`, `place_from`, `confirm`
  (always true), `place_note`, `size_mm` (the manifest's), `origin` (`mating face centre; mate
  along +Y; wires along -Y`), `mate` (the other half's id), `cavities[]` (`cav`, `circuit`, `awg`,
  `colour`, `state`, from the cavities table in cavity order, on both halves), `source`. A plug
  shares its receptacle's place turned half a turn about z, so the two faces meet.
- `parts[]` of `kind: "module"` for every device and common part in devices/manifest.json, one
  per piece (`count`, and each place in `also`): `id` `dev-<key>[-n]`, `device` (the manifest's
  key, DVxx or Pxxx), `node` (the node it sits at), `glb`, `standin`, `cad`, the rest as any part.
- three more nodes: `cargo-bin-passenger` (D-449: the battery, disconnect, Class-T, MRBF holders
  and amplifier, moved there from leg-l4 by `MOVE_DEVICES`), `door-driver`, `door-passenger`.
- `legs[]`: `id` (`L1`-`L4`, `Audio`), `name`, `path[]` (x y z, car frame, from the dash post
  outward), `branches[]` (`to`: a part id, `path[]` starting on a point of the main path),
  `radius_mm`, `conductors`, `housings[]`, `routes[]` (the routes rows whose `leg` is the leg's;
  `Sill` is L4's), `place_from: "provisional"`, `confirm: true`, `note`.
- `unplaced[]` (`device`, `why`): a manifest entry not placed, and why (count 0, or drawn inside
  a board's or a unit's glb). `manifests` (the two manifests' paths).

Where version 2 puts things, all judgement and all `confirm`: a unit's receptacles at its
mock-up's cut-outs, at the receptacle's tip, `+Y` out of the rear face; the dash post's
receptacles (every housing whose `where` is Dash post) as a cluster left of the PMU, a row per
leg, L1 at the top, `CLUSTER_GAP` 20 mm apart, facing the cabin; every other housing's pair, and
every device, on a shelf centred on its node (`NODE_GAP` 5 mm, devices `DEVICE_GAP` 10 mm, rows
wrapping at `ROW_WRAP` 600 mm, kept inside the cabin's width); the dash node's parts on a shelf
right of the PMU. The legs run through `WAYPOINTS` (the firewall grommet on the passenger side at
the firewall's plane, the cowl, the dash rail, the tunnel, the sills, the jamb boots, the hinge
loop), each a fraction across a 00-CAR zone box; `LEGS` says which points each leg and branch
takes, after its routes' from / to / via. A leg carries the housings at the dash post on its row
and every housing at a node it reaches; its radius is from the wired conductors (LIVE or CAPPED)
leaving the post (the Audio leg's, at its far ends): 1.2 x the root of the sum of their squared
diameters, `AWG_OD` a TXL-class catalogue figure (confirm), never under 3 mm. The connectors and
devices come from their builders, run as steps of this command (`--only connectors`, `--only
devices`); `--selftest` also holds version 2: every connector's glb is its family's in the
manifest, mates name each other, no two parts share a place except a mated pair, every leg and
branch has two points or more and every branch starts on its leg's path, every manifest entry is
placed or in `unplaced`.

It reads the record only through `rx7.py export` and writes nothing to it. It is a view of the
design, never a source of facts: a measurement beats the model (R11).

**The devices and the common parts** (`harness/cad/devices/build_devices.py`, D-515, Y28) are
built beside it, into `devices/` under the same folder: one glb per model - the five found online
(the Teensy 4.1, the XIAO, the Waveshare 12.3 in display, Pololu's G2 driver and OptiFuse's
BLR-504 block, each a cad row in 01-REFERENCE) and the stand-ins drawn from the parameters in
its STANDINS, every number with its source - and `devices/manifest.json`, which puts each
device (DVxx) and common part (Pxxx, or another area's qualified) at a node of this scene with
its count, size, origin and cad row. Same frame (mm, z up, the part's own), same writer, same
tessellation; every entry `confirm`. The export (Y26) reads the manifest to place them;
`harness/cad/devices/README.md` has the rest.

**The connectors** (`harness/cad/connectors/build_connectors.py`, D-514, D-515, Y24) are built
the same way into `connectors/`: one glb per DEUTSCH variant the housings name (17), and
`connectors/manifest.json` (committed; the glb files are not). The dimensions are 01-REFERENCE
`connector_dims`, read through `rx7.py sql`; a variant with TE's model on this machine (the three
DT13 headers, on the Mac) is converted from it, the rest are stand-ins drawn from their row
(`standin` in the manifest). Each file's origin is the **mating face's centre** - a plug's front
face, a receptacle's shroud floor - the mate along **+Y**, the wires along **-Y**, z up as the
housing is read, x along the cavity rows; so a mated pair is the receptacle and the plug turned
half a turn about Z, and a housing is placed by putting its origin where it mates. Nodes `body`,
`latch` or `shroud`, `flange`, `cavities`, `wedgelock`. `build_all(out_dir)` returns the manifest
for the export (Y26); `--only`, `--check` and `--selftest` as above.
