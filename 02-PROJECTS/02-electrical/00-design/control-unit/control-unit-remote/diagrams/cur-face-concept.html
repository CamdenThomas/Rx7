CUR face, on a grid

This is your new layout. The screen knob is at the top left with the mirror stick under it, and the two are the same size. Right of them is a grid of keys: the hazard on top, as wide as both winks, then the two winks, then the fuel door and the hatch. The two knobs together are exactly as tall as the three rows of keys, split evenly. One gap size runs everywhere, so the whole face reads as a grid. Every size follows the face's height, and the slider shows how small it can go before something gets hard to press. Ruled as the CUR's face (D-602). The board follows it at the CAD pass (Y71); the box's current plan is cur-plan.svg in this folder.

The face, enlarged

Click a key, the edge of the stick's well to move it, or the middle of the stick to push it. On the screen knob, click its left or right half to turn it, and its middle to click it. Hold the knob's middle for three seconds to start pairing. The scroll wheel also turns the knob.

What the drawing guessed

Everything below was chosen so the sketch would work. Any of it can change.

  1. 1The layout. As you described it: screen knob over mirror stick on the left, then hazard, winks and releases in a grid. It replaced D-595's three columns (D-602). The CUR board is redrawn to it at the CAD pass (Y71), with an I/O expander and an ADC putting the keys and the stick on the I²C bus (D-604), and one DRC 24 on its back (D-597).
  2. 2The height. Starts at 32 mm, about the least where both knobs stay 12 mm or more across. Below about 30.5 mm the knobs go under the floor; the keys stay above theirs down to about 26 mm.
  3. 3The grid. One 1.6 mm gap between every neighbour, across and down, and 2.5 mm of face at every edge. The knobs line up with the grid: the top of the screen knob with the hazard's top, the bottom of the stick's well with the releases' bottom.
  4. 4The keys. Each key is 1.4 times as wide as it is tall, about 11 × 8 mm at 32 mm. The hazard is the two winks' width plus the gap between them, so its edges line up with theirs. The order is a guess: left wink on the left, fuel door under it, hatch on the right.
  5. 5The two knobs. Each is half the key stack's height less one gap: about 12.7 mm across at 32 mm. That is smaller than the CU's 17.4 mm knobs; matching them would make the CUR about 41 mm tall. The screen knob keeps its ticks and carries a light-bulb symbol in its middle that stays upright while the knob turns. The bulb glows at the soft level with the lights on, never brighter, and is dark with them off.
  6. 6The mirror stick. Its well is the same size as the screen knob, and the stick's knob is a little over half of that, about 7 mm. There are no arrows on the well. It moves the left mirror until pushed; after a push it moves the right mirror until 3 s pass with no movement, or until a second push. Nothing on the face shows which mirror it is on.
  7. 7The hazard's light. A red triangle (D-589). It is dull red by day and soft red with the lights on. While latched it flashes bright red with the flasher, about 90 times a minute, and its edge glows red.
  8. 8The other lights. The CU's two levels: every symbol glows softly with the lights on. A wink face lights fully while held, and a release key while pressed.
  9. 9The screen knob's behaviour. As D-580 has it. Turning steps the Display Unit's pages. A click switches to brightness: left brighter, right dimmer, 10 to 100 % in 5 % steps. After 3 s or a second click it goes back to pages. Holding 3 s pairs a phone for 60 s. The four pages, wrapping round, are a guess.
  10. 10Which side is which. The CUR is right of the wheel (D-586). The knobs are drawn on the steering-wheel side, nearest your hand. The button under the drawing moves them to the centre-stack side and keeps the left wink on the left.
