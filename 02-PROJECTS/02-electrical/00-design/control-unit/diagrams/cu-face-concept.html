Control Unit face, one short row

Every size follows the face's height, so the slider under the drawing shows how short it can go before something gets too small to press. Ruled as the CU's face (D-602). About real size, under the stereo

This drawing is set in millimetres, so on most screens it shows at about real size. Hold a ruler to it to check your screen. The stereo above it is an outline of the Pioneer DMH-W4660NEX's face from Pioneer's published size, 171 × 97 mm. It is not a drawing of the unit itself.

What the screen shows

The screen is the same as in the last pass. At rest it shows everything the centre knob sets, small, with no labels. While you turn the knob it shows only the one thing being set, large. It goes back to rest three seconds after the last click. The new view is the dash-light level: hold the knob's push and turn it (D-578). Hold the push and let go without turning, and the climate switches off: the blower and the compressor stop and the screen goes completely dark. The seats still work. 12

In the dark

Here the lights are on, recirculate and the rear-glass defogger are on, the driver seat is cooling at level 2 and the passenger seat is heating at level 1. With the lights on, each key's symbol glows softly. A key whose function is on is fully lit and its edge glows. The ring around the centre knob glows at the same soft level as the symbols and never brighter: the screen shows what the knob is doing. It lights only itself and no longer spills onto the keys. 5

A seat knob, four states 7

The knob is off in the middle and does not push. The six lights sit in a tight arc over the top of the knob. The three on the left are cooling and the three on the right are heating. Each click lights one more, outward from the middle.

What a short face means for the box behind it

What the face set up for the boards, and how it was settled. The boards are redrawn to it at the CAD pass (Y70).

Width. A 171 mm face leaves about 164 mm for the box behind it once the face overlaps its edges. The CU carrier is drawn 180 mm wide and the face board 180 mm, so both get narrower when they are redrawn (Y70). D-542 named exactly this as the cost of matching the stereo's width.

Height. The box behind the face is about 50 mm tall, because the carrier and its 64-way header stand about 49 mm (D-594). A face 25 mm tall cannot cover it, so it cannot slide in from the front the way the stereo does (D-481). Settled (D-604, (a)): the box stays about 50 mm tall, stepping up behind the dash, and is fitted from behind; the connectors are unchanged. Its comfort feed comes in on its own 2-way plug beside the header (D-612).

What the drawing guessed

Everything below was chosen so the sketch would work. Any of it can change.

  1. 1The width, 171 mm. This is the published width of the DMH-W4660NEX's face (the "nose"), from Pioneer's manual. Its chassis behind the face is 178 mm. Nothing has been measured, so check it when the stereo is in hand. The controls are spread to fill the width, with 3 mm left at each end.
  2. 2The height. Starts at one inch, 25.4 mm, with 1.6 mm of face above and below the tallest control. The slider runs from 22 to 32 mm and every control is sized from it.
  3. 3The screen. Two dots wide for every dot tall, a 128 × 64 display as before. Its window is the height of the key stack less a 0.8 mm bezel: about 41 × 20.6 mm at one inch, roughly a 1.8 inch display. No part has been chosen. A wide 256 × 64 display would fill more of the row, if you want the screen bigger rather than the gaps.
  4. 4The four keys. 11 mm wide, stacked 0.8 mm apart, together exactly the screen's height. At one inch each key is about 5 mm tall. The order from the top is a guess: fan mode, recirculate, rear-glass defog, mirror defog.
  5. 5The key lights. With the lights on, a key's symbol glows at about 40 % and its edge faintly. While its function is on, the symbol is at full brightness and the edge glows, with the lights on or off. "The lights" is taken as the park and head lamps, the same as the rest of the dash.
  6. 6The "joystick". Read as the centre knob, since it is the control with the light ring. It still turns and pushes, and its ring is 1.4 mm wide, lit only with the lights and only at the soft level of the other symbols; it has no bright state, because the screen shows what the knob is setting. All three knobs are the same size: 17.4 mm across at one inch, up to 19 mm on a taller face. If you meant a real joystick in place of the knob, say so.
  7. 7The seat knobs. The same size as the centre knob, with the six level lights moved into a tight arc just above the knob, 1.6 mm dots, since there is no room for the old arc. They still turn only, off in the middle.
  8. 8The window switches. A sprung paddle in an 8.5 mm wide slot as tall as the face allows. The paddle is 7 × 8 mm with the window symbol on it, pushed up or down and springing back.
  9. 9The spacing. The same gap between every pair of neighbours, 5 mm at one inch: window, seat, screen, keys, knob, seat, window. The screen and keys are wider than the knob, so the middle three sit slightly left of centre.
  10. 10The "easy to press" floors. A key at least 4.5 mm tall. A knob at least 12 mm across. A screen at least 15 mm tall so the small numbers stay readable from the seat. These are judgement, not measured; try a 4.5 mm key and a 12 mm knob in hand before trusting them.
  11. 11The box behind. Neither way of covering the 50 mm box is chosen; see the section above. Nothing is redrawn until the face is settled.
  12. 12Climate off. A hold is taken as 0.7 s or more. Holding and letting go without turning switches the climate off: the blower and the compressor stop and the screen goes dark. The fan-mode and recirculate keys go dark too, with no soft glow even with the lights on. The seats, both defoggers and the windows keep working. While it is off, turning the knob does nothing, and a click or another hold turns it back on at the last settings. Holding and turning still sets the dash lights; the screen lights only to show the level, then goes dark again. This keeps D-578, which reads "hold its push and turning it". The hold is now hold-and-turn rather than hold, then turn. Recorded once the face is settled.
