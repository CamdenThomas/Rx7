**D-251 — Wire is control, CAN is telemetry. Anything the PMU acts on for the engine or for safety sits on a hardwired PMU input; anything it only logs or displays may arrive on CAN2; and a CAN value ever admitted to a control expression fails open on timeout.** Camden's call, 2026-09-07, the electrical half of `Q-116`. Bus priority (a lower identifier wins arbitration) solves latency under contention — at CAN2's ~1.5 % load the worst case is one lost arbitration, about a quarter of a millisecond — and solves nothing about *source failure*: a rebooting, hung or unplugged ICU simply stops sending, and the PMU cannot tell "no message" from "no pressure". A gate that cuts on timeout kills the pump when a module hiccups; a gate that ignores timeout is silently absent whenever the module is down. Neither is an interlock. So the oil-pressure gate lives on A7 (D-249); the rpm term D-183 waits for arrives as a *secondary* condition whose timeout reads as running; and the PMU logs the ICU's rpm, oil pressure, water temperature and fuel level as received channels for display and cross-check only. *Consequence for the harness:* **fuel level becomes the ICU's signal.** `DP-ICU-B 3` taps the fuel node (`N64`), the ICU publishes it on CAN2 (luxury D-306: frame `0x218`, 2 Hz), and the PMU records it as a received channel. Three readers on the oil node (`N47` — the factory gauge, the PMU's A7, the ICU) is deliberate: a cross-check, not a duplicate, and a disagreement is a sender or wiring fault nothing else would show.
