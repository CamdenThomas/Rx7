**D-276 — Five sheets are cut from the harness model beside the master: one per leg, beginning to end, and the dash node.** Camden, 2026-09-08: four leg diagrams — rear, dash, front, engine — that start at the leg connector at the PMU / relay / fuse box and end at the parts, with fewer overlapping wires; the rear leg to extend to the dash always-hot busbar with the power backbone to that point. His answers to the four design questions: a leg sheet begins at the leg's post housings with each cavity labelled with what feeds it (no PMU / fuse / relay nodes drawn); the dash node is a fifth sheet of its own; the rear sheet owns everything that starts at the battery or the rear stud (Class-T → busbar, disconnect → rear stud, the 1/0 starter run and the 1/0 cranking return, the sill node) and the engine sheet owns the bay's cables with 'from the battery' stubs; straight-run routing. How: `harness.py` builds one model and `subset()` cuts each sheet by conductor group — `LEG-Lx` and `GND-Lx` for a leg, `DASH-NODE` for the node, backbone cables by id — so a conductor is never typed twice. Bundles are per housing (the housing a leg conductor leaves, the housing a node conductor lands on), which is what took the crossings out: each bundle sits beside its housing and the sheet reads left to right. One deviation from the routing answer: Graphviz's true orthogonal router ignores port positions, so wires would no longer land on their pins — the leg sheets use `polyline` (straight segments with bends) instead; the master and node sheets keep splines because of their density. Outputs: `LEG-L1.svg` … `LEG-L4.svg`, `DASH-NODE.svg`, each with an `.html` twin and its own stamp; `{{harness_status}}` reports all six.
