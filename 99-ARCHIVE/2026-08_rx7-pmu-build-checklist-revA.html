1982 Mazda RX-7 FB / GS · Electrical Rebuild

Build Checklist

PMU panel · power backbone · harness — 139 steps

SCOPE PCU, battery, harness
EXCLUDES DCU · cluster · A/C controller · LS
EFFORT 341–553 hrs
MATERIALS $5,230–7,045

Companion to the Rev A pin plan. Phases 0–2 are laptop and apartment work — do them during semesters. Phases 3–8 need the shop. The car drives on the factory harness until step 116, and drives home at the end of every session after that.

PHASE 0

Design & documentation

55–85 hrs · laptop

Nothing gets cut, stripped, or ordered until this phase is signed off.

  1. 001Print the Rev A pin plan. Start a shop binder.
  2. 002Download the 1982 FB factory wiring diagram and the FSM electrical section.
  3. 003Photograph the entire existing harness in place — wide shots, then a close-up of every connector, branch, and ground point.
  4. 004Number every existing connector with tape; log location and function in a sheet.
  5. 005Log every previous-owner hack, splice, and your own taps including the wideband.You will rediscover these mid-build otherwise, always at the worst moment.
  6. 006Photograph and log every existing chassis ground point.
  7. 007Clamp-meter every load on the running car. Headlights low and high, blower each speed, wiper low and high, defog, fuel pump, horn, tail, brake, turn, reverse, interior. Record inrush and steady state.This is the single most valuable hour in the whole project — it sets every wire gauge and every soft fuse. It cannot be done after the harness comes out.
  8. 008Measure pop-up motor stall current, both sides.
  9. 009Obtain the Sicma 39-position housing drawing or the physical part; confirm cavity geometry against the numeric map in Rev A.
  10. 010Build the master circuit spreadsheet: circuit ID, load, measured A, inrush A, gauge, base color, tracer, PMU channel, bulkhead:cavity, route, length.
  11. 011Cross-check: all 39 cavities have a destination.
  12. 012Cross-check: every relay has a coil source, a terminal 30 feed, and a load path.
  13. 013Calculate resistor ladder values — turn stalk, wiper stalk, headlight switch, key, pop-up L, pop-up R, door pins. Target at least 40 ADC counts of separation between states.
  14. 014Pick values from the E24 1% series; write the expected ADC reading for each switch position.
  15. 015Measure each harness route with string. Add 15% for service loops and routing slack.
  16. 016Build the wire cut list: gauge, color, length, quantity.
  17. 017Build the connector BOM: housings, wedge locks, contacts plus 20% spare, seals, sealing plugs.
  18. 018Draw the panel layout 1:1 on paper — PMU, relay bank, fuse block, busbars, diode network, bulkhead positions, and clearance for the 39-pin lever.
  19. 019Mock up the dash structure and verify the panel physically fits with service access.
  20. 020Draw the diode-OR wake network schematic with part numbers.
  21. 021Draw the pop-up H-bridge relay schematic — terminals 30/85/86/87/87a labeled, limit switch interaction shown.
  22. 022Draw the window H-bridge relay schematic.
  23. 023Draw the constant-bus schematic: head unit keep-alive and diag port, each with its fuse value.
  24. 024Freeze the design. Print everything. Date and version it.
GateIf any of steps 011, 012, or 015 fail, you are not done with Phase 0. Fixing a pin allocation on paper costs minutes; fixing it after crimping costs a housing.
PHASE 1

Order & practice

10–16 hrs · apartment
  1. 025Order the PMU first — longest lead time.
  2. 026Order the Ionic S9H. It has been waitlist-only; get in line even if you're months out.
  3. 027Place one consolidated wire and connector order.One large order beats five small ones on both shipping and minimum-length pricing.
  4. 028Order any tools you don't have: Deutsch crimper, AMPSEAL crimper, hydraulic lug crimper, DMM with DC clamp, label printer.
  5. 029Order two spare Sicma housings and extra terminals.You will ruin at least one terminal. Having spares means it isn't a two-week stall.
  6. 030Inventory everything against the BOM on arrival, before starting anything.
  7. 031Build crimp coupons: three each of every terminal and gauge combination you'll use.
  8. 032Pull-test every coupon. Record the force. Adjust technique until it repeats.
  9. 033Practice Deutsch contact insertion and extraction ten times until it's muscle memory.
  10. 034Practice adhesive-lined heat shrink until you get consistent adhesive weep at both ends.
  11. 035Set up the label printer and lock in a label format.
PHASE 2

Bench mule & configuration

35–55 hrs · apartment

The step most first-timers skip. Learn the software with nothing at stake.

  1. 036Build a plywood board, roughly 24 × 36 in.
  2. 037Mount a small ground bus and a fused battery feed on the board.
  3. 038Mount the PMU using a spare housing — not the real one.
  4. 039Terminate a short test pigtail: stud feed, pin 25 ground, pin 7, pin 15, CAN1 pair, six assorted outputs, four inputs.
  5. 040Fit a 120 Ω terminator on CAN1. It has no internal termination — the bus will not come up without it.
  6. 041Install the PMU client on a Windows machine or VM.
  7. 042Connect the USB-to-CAN adapter, power up, confirm the PMU appears.
  8. 043Create the project file. Name every channel to match the Rev A schedule exactly.
  9. 044Wire a headlight bulb to one output. Verify switching and the live current readout.
  10. 045Deliberately short that output. Verify the soft fuse trips. Test retry counts and reset behavior.
  11. 046Configure PWM on an LED strip. Tune the theater fade curve until you like it.
  12. 047Build one physical resistor ladder on a scrap multi-position switch.
  13. 048Read the ADC value at each position. Compare to your calculation. Adjust resistors.
  14. 049Write the decode table into the config as a lookup.
  15. 050Repeat 047–049 for all six ladder types.
  16. 051Configure turn signal flash logic: frequency, hazard override, bulb-out detection from current draw.
  17. 052Configure wiper logic: LOW, HIGH, intermittent timer, park sensing, O8 braking.
  18. 053Configure headlight logic from the ladder — PARK feeds O6 only, HEAD adds O2.
  19. 054Configure the keep-alive latch: pin 7 diode-OR sources, O22 self-hold, shutdown delay.
  20. 055Configure crank logic and interlocks on O21.
  21. 056Configure fuel pump prime and run behavior.
  22. 057Configure the CAN keypad: defog, interior override, spare keys, backlight.
  23. 058Set undervoltage and overvoltage warnings appropriate to the lithium's BMS window.
  24. 059Configure the built-in inertia switch.
  25. 060Enable data logging on the channels you care about.
  26. 061Save the config. Back it up. Start a version log — RevA-01, -02, and so on.
  27. 062Full bench dry-run: exercise every configured function with real switches and real loads.
PHASE 3

Power backbone

18–28 hrs · shop

Factory harness untouched. The car is better at the end of this weekend than it was at the start.

  1. 063Disconnect the factory battery.
  2. 064Fabricate and mount the battery box in the rear compartment.
  3. 065Mount the Ionic S9H. Secure it against real g-loading in every axis, not just downward.
  4. 066Mount the master disconnect within reach.
  5. 067Mount the Class-T holder as close to the battery positive as physically possible.
  6. 068Cut and hydraulically crimp the battery cables. Adhesive shrink and a boot on every lug.
  7. 069Install the rear ground stud: grind to bare metal, star washer, torque to spec, seal with cavity wax.
  8. 070Run the 4 AWG main feed forward through the tunnel. Grommet at every pass-through, abrasion loom the full length.
  9. 071Terminate the forward end on a temporary insulated junction post.
  10. 072Install the front star ground node.
  11. 073Reconnect the factory harness to the new feed and ground.
  12. 074Start the car. Verify charging voltage at the battery.
  13. 075Voltage-drop test while cranking — battery post to starter, and the ground return. Record both.
  14. 076Drive it. Should be identical to before, on an entirely new backbone.
PHASE 4

Panel build

45–70 hrs · bench
  1. 077Cut and fold the aluminum backer plate per the 1:1 drawing.
  2. 078Deburr, drill, and finish the plate.
  3. 079Mount the PMU, standing it off the plate for airflow.
  4. 080Mount 16 relay sockets. Populate 10.The six empty sockets are the future-proofing. They cost almost nothing now.
  5. 081Mount the fuse block.
  6. 082Mount the always-hot busbar and the ground bus.
  7. 083Mount the bulkhead connector brackets for C1 through C7.
  8. 084Run the stud feed from busbar to PMU stud. Torque to spec.
  9. 085Run pin 25 ground to the ground bus — the shortest, heaviest path on the plate.Every inductive load's flyback current comes back through this one wire.
  10. 086Wire the relay bank per the H-bridge schematics. Label every relay position on the plate itself.
  11. 087Build the diode-OR wake network on a terminal strip. Label every input to it.
  12. 088Build the constant bus — head unit keep-alive and diag port, each individually fused.
  13. 089Terminate the real 39-pin housing. All 39 cavities. Work in cavity order, checking off each on the printed schedule as it seats.
  14. 090Insert the wedge lock. Verify no contact backs out under a light pull.
  15. 091Route each wire from the 39-pin to its bulkhead connector and terminate.
  16. 092Terminate reserved wires into their bulkhead cavities and cap the far ends.
  17. 093Fit sealing plugs only where there is genuinely no wire. There should be almost none.
  18. 094Continuity-test every path: 39-pin cavity to bulkhead cavity. Check off all 39, plus every relay leg.
  19. 095High-resistance check between adjacent power cavities.
  20. 096Label every wire at both ends and every bulkhead connector.
  21. 097Bench-power the panel from a current-limited supply.
  22. 098Verify each output appears at the correct bulkhead pin with a test lamp.
  23. 099Verify each input reads correctly by shorting its bulkhead pin to ground.
  24. 100Photograph the finished panel from every angle for the build log.
GateThe 39-pin does not get opened again after step 090. If step 094 finds a fault, fix it now — this is your last cheap opportunity.
PHASE 5

Harness segments

90–150 hrs · bench

One segment at a time, in order: C3 → C2 → C4 → C1 → C5 → C6 → C7.

  1. 101Lay the segment's route out full-scale on the board using your measured lengths.
  2. 102Cut every wire to length per the cut list, plus service loop.
  3. 103Strip, crimp, and seat contacts in the mating bulkhead housing.
  4. 104Fit the wedge lock. Pull-test every contact.
  5. 105Terminate the device ends — bulb sockets, motors, switches, senders.
  6. 106Continuity-test every wire end to end.
  7. 107Tug-test every crimp.
  8. 108Label both ends of every wire, and the segment itself.
  9. 109Bundle with Tesa tape or loom, branching at the right points. Do not final-wrap.
  10. 110Bag and tag the finished segment. Return to 101 for the next one.
PHASE 6

Install & migrate

55–90 hrs · shop

Hard rule: the car drives home at the end of every session. Never start a cutover you can't finish or reverse before dark.

  1. 111Mount the panel in the dash. Verify the 39-pin lever and all seven bulkheads are reachable in place.
  2. 112Connect the main feed and panel ground. Connect no bulkhead yet.
  3. 113Power up in the car with all outputs disabled. Confirm CAN comms from the diag port.
  4. 114Install segment C3. Flip every switch and verify the entire input layer reads correctly — still zero outputs enabled.You can validate every input in the car before a single load is connected. Use that.
  5. 115Install the remaining segments physically — routed, secured, grommeted — but leave every load on the factory harness.
  6. 116Begin migration in this order: interior and hatch lamps → lighter and USB → horn → wipers and washer → tail/park/marker/plate → brake → turn L and R → reverse → defog → headlights → pop-ups → windows → blower → fuel pump → ignition → start.
  7. 117For each circuit: unplug the load from the factory harness at the factory connector.
  8. 118Tape the factory end back and label it MIGRATED with the date.
  9. 119Connect the load to the new harness.
  10. 120Enable that one output with the current limit set just above the value from step 007.
  11. 121Operate the load. Read live current in the client. Set the soft fuse from the measured value, not the estimate.
  12. 122Voltage-drop test at the device under load. Under 0.5 V.
  13. 123Log it: migrated / verified / measured A / soft fuse set / V-drop.
  14. 124Before you stop for the day, confirm the car starts and drives. Every day.
  15. 125After ignition and start migrate, do a full function check of every circuit in one pass.
PHASE 7

Factory harness removal

8–14 hrs · shop
  1. 126Confirm every circuit on the migration sheet is checked off.
  2. 127Drive for a week with the factory harness disconnected but still physically in the car.
  3. 128Pull the factory harness out intact. Do not cut it.
  4. 129Board it in the shop as a reference. Keep it until after shakedown is complete.
PHASE 8

Shakedown

25–45 hrs · road
  1. 130Cold start test. Watch the Ionic heater behavior and log battery voltage through the crank.
  2. 131Night drive: headlights, high beams, pop-ups, wink, every lighting circuit.
  3. 132Wet drive: wipers in all modes, washer, defog.
  4. 133Long drive with logging on. Review the log for current spikes, voltage sag, and any soft fuse sitting near a trip.
  5. 134Raise any soft fuse that sits too close to real draw. Lower any that's needlessly generous.
  6. 135Re-torque every stud and lug after heat cycling.
  7. 136Re-check voltage drop on the main feed and ground.
  8. 137Now do the final loom wrap, tie-down, and grommet sealing.
  9. 138Back up the final config, print it, file it with the migration sheet.
  10. 139Put a spare set of Deutsch contacts, a wedge tool, and a printed pinout in the car.
